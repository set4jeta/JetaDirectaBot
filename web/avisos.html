What the alert that reaches your Discord looks like

In short

  • A message in your channel with the champion, role, rank, team and all ten participants, as soon as the game starts.
  • The bot checks the accounts every 30 seconds: that is the maximum delay.
  • It works in a server channel or in your private chat, without depending on any server.

What information does the alert carry?

This is the shape of the message, with a real player from the bot's database. On Discord it arrives as an embed with the player's photo and the logo of their team.

How do you turn it on?

  1. You add the bot. One click and the bot joins your server. It asks for no admin permission and does not read history.
  2. You pick a league and a channel. /leagues lec and /subscribe in the channel where you want the alerts.
  3. That is it. There is nothing else to do. The alert shows up on its own when someone queues up.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the alert take to arrive?

The bot checks the accounts every 30 seconds, so the alert arrives at most 30 seconds after the game shows up in Riot's API. The game itself is already a little under way when it appears: that is Riot's timing, not the bot's.

Why does the game clock in the alert not match the one in the client?

Because the clock the API returns is the spectator server's, which runs about three minutes behind and starts in negative numbers. The alert shows the real time and tells you separately when you can spectate, instead of printing the raw number and looking broken.

Do I need to be a server admin?

To choose the alert channel, yes: it needs the Manage Server permission. To get them in your private chat, no: you add the bot to your account and it works anywhere, without depending on any server.

Can I follow a single player instead of a whole league?

Yes. With /seguir and the pro's name you get only their games in your private chat; with a league name, every game in that league.

What happens if Riot's API goes down?

Games stop being detected until it comes back, and /health says which source is failing and when it last updated. No alerts are made up and old ones are not resent.
